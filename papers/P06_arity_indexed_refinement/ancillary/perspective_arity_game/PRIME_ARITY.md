# Prime arities from distinction capacity

This result continues the complete all-arity capacity family. Primes are not
inserted as arithmetic generators and no Euler product is assumed.

## 1. Transparent arity

For a presented arity \(q\ge2\), call the capacity step **transparent in its
own residue system** when

\[
\boxed{
\binom qk\equiv0\pmod q
\qquad(0<k<q).
}
\]

Equivalently, the \(q\)-resultant expansion has only its two endpoint terms
after reduction by \(q\):

\[
(1+y)^q\equiv1+y^q\pmod q.
\]

This definition uses the capacities generated by a \(q\)-resultant set. It
does not begin with divisibility of an arbitrary integer into factors.

## 2. Prime-arity theorem

An arity is transparent exactly when it is prime:

\[
\boxed{
q\text{ transparent}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q\text{ prime}.}
\]

### Proof

If \(q\) is prime, then for \(0<k<q\), the numerator of

\[
\binom qk=\frac{q(q-1)\cdots(q-k+1)}{k!}
\]

contains \(q\), while \(k!\) does not; hence \(q\mid\binom qk\).

Conversely, let \(q\) be composite and let \(p<q\) be a prime divisor of
\(q\). Then

\[
\binom qp=\frac qp\binom{q-1}{p-1}.
\]

Modulo \(p\), the second factor is

\[
\binom{q-1}{p-1}
\equiv\binom{-1}{p-1}
=(-1)^{p-1}\not\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

The factor \(q/p\) is therefore missing one factor of \(p\) needed for
divisibility by \(q\). Thus \(q\nmid\binom qp\), so \(q\) is not
transparent. ∎

Prime arities have consequently emerged as the arities whose own distinction
capacity propagates without intermediate self-residues.

## 3. Prime-capacity digit theorem

Let \(p\) be a transparent arity and write

\[
n=\sum_{j\ge0}d_jp^j,
\qquad0\le d_j<p.
\]

Repeated transparency gives

\[
(1+y)^n
\equiv\prod_j(1+y^{p^j})^{d_j}\pmod p.
\]

Every combination of lower places has total exponent at most \(p^j-1\), so
the coefficient of \(y^{p^j}\) is exactly \(d_j\). Hence

\[
\boxed{
\binom n{p^j}\bmod p=d_j.
}

The same incidence-capacity construction that recovered binary identity now
recovers the complete base-\(p\) identity for every transparent arity.

## 4. One-place character fibre and its diagonal lift

In a \(P_1\) complex presentation, let \(\omega=e^{2\pi i/p}\). Retaining
every residue character \(r=0,1,\ldots,p-1\), the finite \(J\)-place carrier
is

\[
\boxed{
T_{p,r,J}(z)
=\prod_{j=0}^{J-1}
\left(
1+\omega^rz^{p^j}+\omega^{2r}z^{2p^j}
+\cdots+\omega^{(p-1)r}z^{(p-1)p^j}
\right).
}
\]

No nontrivial \(r\) is selected as native to one unordered atomic
\(p\)-resultant relation; its complete one-place character fibre is retained.
Using the same \(r\) at every generated place gives the diagonal lift above.
Every nontrivial diagonal member has the self-similarity

\[
T_{p,r}(z)
=\left(\sum_{d=0}^{p-1}\omega^{rd}z^d\right)T_{p,r}(z^p).
\]

As in the binary case, its local factors have roots on the unit circle at
unbounded \(p\)-power scales. Their union is dense, giving the unit circle as
a natural boundary for every nontrivial diagonal digit character.

### Completeness correction across places

For \(J>1\), the complete character group of the interacting digit places is
not indexed by one repeated \(r\). It is indexed independently by

\[
\mathbf r=(r_0,\ldots,r_{J-1})\in(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^J.
\]

Thus the displayed one-index product retains a \(p\)-member diagonal inside a
\(p^J\)-member full fibre. `CROSS_BOUNDARY_COMPLETION.md` repairs this
omission, proves full-fibre orthogonality, and uses its complete boundary
trace. The diagonal self-similarity and natural-boundary result remain valid
for the diagonal members; they are no longer mislabeled as the entire
multi-place fibre.

## 5. What has and has not emerged

The sole distinction closure has now generated:

1. a criterion selecting prime arities without assuming prime factorization;
2. base-\(p\) identity from prime-arity incidence capacities;
3. the complete one-place fibre and its self-similar diagonal lifts; and
4. their common analytic boundary.

The later arithmetic branch derives, rather than inserts, the multiplicative
cost character. The cross-boundary branch then retains the full multi-place
character groups and derives their boundary interaction.
