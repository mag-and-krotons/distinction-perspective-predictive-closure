preserves incidence.  Let \(\mathscr C_q\) be its connected forms.  Unique

\[
\boxed{\mathscr H_q\simeq\operatorname{Sym}(\mathscr C_q).}

## 3. The identity-and-symmetry-preserving realization

Retain all labelled presentations and then remove only redundant names.  A
native form \(H\) with automorphism group \(\operatorname{Aut}(H)\) receives
the forced orbit weight

\[
\frac1{|\operatorname{Aut}(H)|}.
\]

Indeed, if \(H\) has \(m\) events and \(n\) resultants, then the number of
labelled presentations in its orbit is

\[
\frac{m!n!}{|\operatorname{Aut}(H)|}.
\]

Define the two formal bivariate shadows

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal H_q(u,x)
&=1+\sum_{[H]}
\frac{u^{e(H)}x^{v(H)}}{|\operatorname{Aut}(H)|},\\
\mathcal C_q(u,x)
&=\sum_{[C]\text{ connected}}
\frac{u^{e(C)}x^{v(C)}}{|\operatorname{Aut}(C)|}.
\end{aligned}
\]

When \(r\) equal connected components occur, their automorphism group also
contains the exchange group on the \(r\) copies.  Their coefficient is
therefore divided by \(r!\).  Consequently component decomposition gives

\[
\boxed{\mathcal H_q(u,x)=\exp\!\bigl(\mathcal C_q(u,x)\bigr).}
\]

This is not an assertion that \(P_q\) internally counts with rational
numbers.  It is the \(P_1\) realization obtained by preserving the complete
label fibre and its symmetries.

## 5. Closed symmetry carrier and an emergent analytic boundary

For \(n\) labelled resultants, one labelled atomic event may choose any of
\(\binom nq\) incidences. Thus \(m\) labelled events have
\(\binom nq^m\) presentations before unused resultants are removed. Summing
over labelled event sets and applying the same inclusion-exclusion as above
gives the exact formal carrier

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal H_q(u,x)
=e^{-x}\sum_{n\ge0}
e^{u\binom nq}\frac{x^n}{n!}.
}
\]

The connected carrier is forced formally by

\[
\boxed{\mathcal C_q(u,x)=\log\mathcal H_q(u,x).}
\]

For every \(q\ge2\), fixing \(u\) produces a sharp analytic phase in the
\(x\)-series:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{ll}
\Re u\le0 &: \text{infinite radius of convergence},\\
\Re u>0 &: \text{zero radius of convergence}.
\end{array}}
\]

Indeed, the \(n\)-th summand has magnitude governed by
\(\exp(\Re(u)\binom nq)/n!\). Its \(n\)-th root tends to zero when
\(\Re u\le0\) and to infinity when \(\Re u>0\), because
\(\binom nq\) grows like \(n^q/q!\). The nonzero factor \(e^{-x}\) does not
change the radius.

The coordinates \(u\) and \(x\) are \(P_1\) event/resultant audit
coordinates, not time or space. Nevertheless this is a new law generated by
interaction and the complete symmetry realization: one half-plane supports

\[

The first has a geometric denominator, coordinate inversion, poles, and a

\boxed{
